The problem

Search answered the topic, not the question.

The old search matched words. It never knew who was asking or what they needed, so every role got the same generic report from a process no one could inspect.

The research

I put the model through the same research I put people through.

On the model: mock-real queries from researcher interviews. Before answering, it picks who it’s writing for, and it got six of seven right. The seventh question fit two readers at once, so it asked instead of guessing.

On people: researchers wanted to ask in their own words, watch the research as it runs, and check where every claim came from.

The design lives where the two met: when the model can’t be sure, it asks. A good support conversation makes the same first move.

The solution

Designed as a researcher’s journey: ask, scope, watch the work, check the receipts.

I designed Deeli to behave like a research assistant. Three of the four steps meet expectations researchers told us about. The second exists because of the one test the model couldn’t pass.

01 · Ask

Talk to it like a person.

Take the question, not the keyword

I rebuilt the input to take the whole ask and its intent, not the words that happen to overlap.

02 · Scope

Agree on the brief first.

Aim the report

I made the product confirm who the report is for and what it should cover before researching.

03 · Research

Watch the research happen.

Show the agents

I surfaced the multi-agent research as it runs, so the work is visible, not magic.

04 · Verify

Check any claim’s source.

Make claims inspectable

I tied every statement back to its source, so the report survives the check.

Method · How I worked with AI

The story above ran on a working method.

Three habits carried this project from first measurement to shipped interface.

persona.md · who we test for

I build persona.md from interviews, user feedback, and usage data; it also feeds our product-prioritization flow. Upkeep that took 6+ hours a week now takes about one.

Measured, not eyeballed

An AI judge grades every answer against the persona questions. Review stops arguing taste and starts asking which check failed. The flagged case above is one of its findings.

Primitives the model can adapt

I write the interface as a few design primitives in design.md. The AI adapts to cases we never drew, and the doc itself is the handoff: engineers read it instead of meeting.

Post-launch

28% of queries were re-asks. We’re finding out why.

Each re-ask raises one question: did the answer miss, or did the topic have a second angle? The two need opposite fixes, so the next move is research, not a feature.
